What we found

Every result is reported as it came out, including the ones that went against the model.

Two cameras beat one

On M3FD, fusion beats the best single camera by 4.0 mAP. A plain concatenation baseline already captures 3.4 of that.

The gated scan adds a small margin

Over three seeds CFFM-Net leads the matched baseline by 0.6 ± 0.4 mAP on M3FD (p = 0.11) and 0.8 ± 1.1 on LLVIP (p = 0.37). Neither is statistically significant, and the paper says so.

Ahead when the thermal camera fails

Across eleven injected faults on M3FD the model leads by 3.6 points on thermal-side faults and trails by 0.9 on visible-side ones.

Fault training beats architecture

A concatenation baseline trained with fault augmentation is more robust than the model: 5.1 points ahead on M3FD and 11.9 on LLVIP, averaged over the eleven faults. Training the scan the same way is the next experiment.

Architecture

The network as trained, drawn with the real activations of one LLVIP test pair. The two backbones never mix. They meet only in the fusion blocks, where each token’s scan step is scaled by how reliable its camera is there.

Four phases

Still images first, then memory across frames and tracking, then live cameras, then several cameras combined into anonymous counts. All four are built, and every number comes from the result files in the repository.
