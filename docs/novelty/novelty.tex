\begin{quote}
\small
\textsc{Abstract.} The pilot builds and evaluates the still-image form of \method{}:
a dual-stream YOLO26 detector whose visible and thermal features are fused by a
selective state-space scan. In that scan, the discretisation step of every token is
scaled by an estimate of how reliable its modality is at its location. This note
states the problem precisely and identifies twelve recent papers whose limitations
bear on it. It separates the limitations their authors state from those observed in
their reported results. It then specifies five contributions of the pilot, each tied
to at least one of those limitations and to an experiment that can refute it. The
pilot addresses six stated limitations from five papers. Two further stated
limitations, temporal memory and post-export accuracy, are deferred to later phases
and listed as such. Claims are framed as hypotheses. A two-day pilot with one
training seed per configuration can support or undermine them, but cannot establish
them.
\end{quote}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------
\section{The problem}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

Detecting very small objects with visible and thermal cameras is the regime in which
each sensor fails differently. Visible imagery loses objects in darkness, glare and
fog. Thermal imagery loses texture and confuses objects with backgrounds at the same
temperature. Both lose objects that cover only a few pixels. Three properties are
therefore needed together:
\begin{itemize}
  \item enough spatial resolution to see objects of a few pixels;
  \item a fusion rule that trusts each sensor only where it is informative;
  \item a cost low enough for real-time use.
\end{itemize}
State-space models (SSMs) are attractive because they mix information globally at
linear cost~\citep{gu2023mamba,liu2024vmamba}. The recent literature has shown,
however, that dropping a Mamba block into a detector is neither cheap nor clearly
useful (Section~\ref{sec:lims}). The pilot asks a narrower question: \emph{can a
selective scan be made to fuse two sensors selectively, at the resolution where small
objects live, at a cost compatible with a real-time YOLO detector?}

Two patterns stand out. First, the cost of SSM blocks is the most frequently reported
weakness. It is measured in CFMW, stated in UAVDet and stated in general terms by
SF-Mamba. Second, small and tiny objects remain the acknowledged failure case of the
best recent detectors, with or without an SSM. Fusion work also assumes, almost
everywhere, that both sensors are equally trustworthy at every pixel. The
fusion-weighting schemes of CFMW and Fusion-Mamba act on the output of the scan,
after a degraded modality has already written into the state.

\subsection*{C1. Reliability-gated cross-modal selective scan}

\paragraph{What it is}
At every location, a small head estimates a reliability $r^m\in(0,1)$ for each
modality $m$ from the two feature maps and their absolute difference. The visible and
thermal tokens of each location are \emph{interleaved} in one sequence, so the hidden
state alternates between modalities at every position. The selective step of token
$k$ is then scaled by the reliability of its modality at its location:
\begin{equation}
  \Delta_k=\operatorname{softplus}(w^{\top}x_k+b)\cdot r^{m(k)}_{p(k)} .
  \label{eq:gate}
\end{equation}
Because $\bar A_k=\exp(\Delta_k A)$ and $\bar B_k\approx\Delta_k B_k$, a token judged
unreliable ($r\to0$) neither writes into the state nor erases
it~\citep{gu2023mamba}. A displacement field resamples thermal features onto the
visible grid before the scan, following COMO~\citep{como2024}. A depthwise
convolutional branch beside the scan restores local two-dimensional structure.

\paragraph{Why it is new}
CFMW and Fusion-Mamba weight the modalities on the \emph{output} of the
scan~\citep{li2025cfmw,dong2025fusionmamba}. MambaRefine-YOLO gates by global
illumination and modality difference~\citep{mambarefine2025}. Gating the
discretisation step per location and per modality controls what enters the state
itself. No published fusion method found in the survey does this.

\paragraph{Limitations addressed}
UAVDet's failure in dark scenes and its untested multimodal setting. C-GSPN's loss of
two-dimensional structure, through interleaving, which keeps the two modalities of
each location adjacent, and through the local branch. Fusion-Mamba's registration
assumption, through offset alignment.

\paragraph{Pilot test}
Three comparisons, all under identical training:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \method{} against the same network with $r\equiv1$ (gating off);
  \item both against a plain two-stream concatenation baseline;
  \item a test-time probe that degrades one modality and shifts the thermal image,
        which requires no extra training.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{C2. A small-object pathway in a real-time dual-stream YOLO}

\paragraph{What it is}
A stride-4 detection head is grafted onto YOLO26~\citep{yolo26_2026}. The SSM fusion
is applied from stride 8, not only at strides 16 and 32 as in
MambaVision~\citep{hatamizadeh2025mambavision}. Evaluation reports AP in the AI-TOD
size bands (2--8, 8--16 and 16--32 pixels) alongside COCO AP\textsubscript{S}.

\paragraph{Limitations addressed}
Small objects missed by CFMW. Objects under ten pixels missed by HPS-DETR.
RGBT-Tiny's warning about IoU-based evaluation, through size-binned reporting now and
SAFit once RGBT-Tiny access is granted.

\paragraph{Pilot test}
Size-binned AP of \method{} against the two-stream baseline with and without the
stride-4 head.

\subsection*{C3. Efficient placement and a portable parallel scan}

\paragraph{What it is}
Two decisions keep the cost of the scan down.
\begin{itemize}
  \item Scans run where sequences are long. Interleaving doubles the sequence length,
        to 10\,240 tokens at stride 8 for a $640\times512$ input. No scan runs at
        stride 4, where a reliability-weighted sum suffices.
  \item The selective scan is implemented twice and tested for numerical agreement:
        with the official CUDA kernel where available, and as a two-level chunked
        parallel scan in plain PyTorch. The PyTorch version needs about
        $2\sqrt{L}$ sequential steps instead of $L$, and it runs on Windows laptops
        and on GPUs that the CUDA kernel does not support.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Limitations addressed}
UAVDet's poor GPU parallelism, SF-Mamba's slow short sequences, and the measured cost
of CFMW and Fusion-Mamba.

\paragraph{Pilot test}
Parameters, GFLOPs and batch-one latency on a T4 for every model, including NMS where
it is used.

\subsection*{C4. A necessity test for the SSM}

\paragraph{What it is}
A control model replaces the selective scan in CMFM with a gated depthwise
convolution of matched cost. Everything else, including reliability gating of the
input, is kept.

\paragraph{Limitation addressed}
MambaOut found the SSM unnecessary for ImageNet classification and left detection
open~\citep{yu2025mambaout}. The analysis of Mamba as gated linear
attention~\citep{han2024mlla} makes the same control necessary.

\paragraph{Pilot test}
\method{} against the control on LLVIP. A null result is reported, not hidden.

\subsection*{C5. A single measurement protocol}

\paragraph{What it is}
All of these run through one code path:
\begin{itemize}
  \item every baseline is retrained, not copied from a paper;
  \item one evaluation script scores every model;
  \item latency is measured on a T4, the GPU used by the YOLO and DETR literature;
  \item every table is generated from logged runs.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Limitation addressed}
The incomparable latency figures of Mamba YOLO. The fifteen internal
inconsistencies found in the four source papers of the project.

\section{Scope of the pilot}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

\paragraph{What the pilot does}
It trains nine configurations at the smallest YOLO26 scale with a short schedule and
one seed each, on LLVIP and M3FD, the two freely downloadable paired benchmarks
(Table~\ref{tab:runs}). The planned total is about 26 T4 GPU-hours, run in parallel on
Kaggle T4s and a local RTX~4050.

\paragraph{What the pilot does not claim}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Temporal memory across frames. That is \methodS{}, which answers MambaST's
        stated limitation in Phase~2.
  \item Robustness to sensor dropout over time, and the full robustness protocol.
  \item Accuracy after TensorRT export on embedded hardware, which answers LCMamNet's
        observation in Phase~3.
  \item Results on RGBT-Tiny, which await dataset approval.
  \item Statistical significance. One seed per configuration cannot separate small
        effects from run-to-run variation, which HPS-DETR's own tables suggest is
        about 1 to 1.5 points~\citep{wang2025hpsdetr}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related work}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

The full survey, newest first, is Chapter~4 of the research dossier. This section
places the pilot within it.

\paragraph{Real-time detection}
The accuracy--latency frontier in 2025--2026 belongs to two lines of work. YOLO26
removes NMS and adds a small-target-aware label assignment~\citep{yolo26_2026}.
Detection transformers draw on DINOv2 and DINOv3 features:
RF-DETR~\citep{robinson2025rfdetr}, RT-DETRv4~\citep{rtdetrv4_2025} and
DEIMv2~\citep{deimv2_2025}. YOLOv12 and YOLOv13 bring attention and hypergraph
correlation into the YOLO family~\citep{tian2025yolov12,lei2025yolov13}. The pilot
builds on YOLO26 because its assignment already targets small objects and its modules
are open to extension.

\paragraph{Vision state-space models}
Several 2025--2026 backbones make SSMs competitive as general backbones:
MambaVision~\citep{hatamizadeh2025mambavision},
Spatial-Mamba~\citep{xiao2025spatialmamba}, DAMamba~\citep{damamba2025} and
PRISMamba~\citep{prismamba2026}. The critical literature is as important.
\begin{itemize}
  \item MambaOut questions whether the SSM is needed at all~\citep{yu2025mambaout}.
  \item MLLA recasts Mamba as gated linear attention~\citep{han2024mlla}.
  \item SF-Mamba and C-GSPN report cost and spatial-structure
        problems~\citep{sfmamba2026,cgspn2026}.
  \item VNCT and GraphScan contest the need for directional
        scans~\citep{vnct2026,graphscan2026}.
\end{itemize}
C3 and C4 respond directly to this critical line.

\paragraph{Mamba inside YOLO}
Mamba YOLO established the Ultralytics-based baseline~\citep{wang2025mambayolo}.
MambaPSA places Mamba inside YOLO26 and reports changes of $-0.1$ to $+0.9$
AP~\citep{mambapsa2026}. ScopeMamba-YOLO, YOLO12-MambaScan and HDMamba-YOLO target UAV
small objects~\citep{scopemamba2026,yolo12mambascan2026,hdmambayolo2026}. UAVDet
shows that most of its gain comes from the stride-4 head and early capacity, not the
Mamba block~\citep{yang2026uavdet}. The pilot therefore does not claim a
Mamba-in-YOLO block as its contribution. The block exists to perform gated fusion.

\paragraph{Small and tiny objects}
SFDNet, Dome-DETR and EFSI-DETR define the current state of the
art~\citep{sfdnet2026,domedetr2025,efsidetr2026}. RGBT-Tiny contributes the
benchmark that matches the full project and a matching measure for tiny
boxes~\citep{ying2025rgbttiny}. HPS-DETR illustrates the value of fusing the stride-4
level~\citep{wang2025hpsdetr}.

\paragraph{Visible--thermal fusion}
Fusion moved from attention, in CFT and ICAFusion~\citep{qingyun2021cft,shen2024icafusion},
to state-space blocks:
Fusion-Mamba~\citep{dong2025fusionmamba}, CFMW~\citep{li2025cfmw},
COMO~\citep{como2024}, WaveMamba~\citep{wavemamba2025},
MS2Fusion~\citep{ms2fusion2025} and MambaRefine-YOLO~\citep{mambarefine2025}. In 2026,
foundation-model fusion followed, with RegisterBridgeMM and
CFGPNet~\citep{registerbridge2026,cfgpnet2026}. Misalignment is handled by offset
guidance in COMO and by contextually guided SSM fusion~\citep{como2024,cgssm2025}.
None gates the state update per modality and location, which is the step C1 adds.

\paragraph{Temporal and streaming detection}
MambaST fuses three visible--thermal frames~\citep{mambast2024}. TMambaDet runs a
bidirectional temporal Mamba offline~\citep{tmambadet2026}. Zubi\'c and colleagues
detect with recurrent SSMs on event cameras~\citep{zubic2024ssmevent}.
Alignment-free visible--thermal video detectors use windows, graphs and hypergraphs
without SSMs~\citep{msenet2024,msgnet2025,dchnet2026}. These define Phase~2, for which
the pilot builds and tests the shared components.

\section{Threats to validity}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{One seed.} Differences below about 1.5 AP are inconclusive and are
        reported as such.
  \item \emph{LLVIP is close to saturated} for AP\textsubscript{50}. The pilot
        reports AP\textsubscript{50:95} and size bands, and adds M3FD.
  \item \emph{Both pilot datasets are registered.} Misalignment is therefore tested
        only by synthetic shifts at test time. Real misalignment waits for
        UVT-VOD2024 and DroneVehicle.
  \item \emph{Short schedules at the smallest scale} may favour simpler models.
        Rankings are confirmed in the full plan at the small (s) scale with three
        seeds.
  \item \emph{Re-implemented baselines} may differ from the originals. Every
        difference is documented in the code.
\end{itemize}
