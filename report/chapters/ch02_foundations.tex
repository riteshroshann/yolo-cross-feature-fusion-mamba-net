\chapter{Technical Foundations}
\label{ch:foundations}

This chapter introduces the ideas the rest of the dossier depends on. It
covers how a YOLO detector is organised and why small objects strain it. It
then derives the selective state-space model behind Mamba and shows how such
models have been adapted to images. It ends with visible--thermal fusion and
the step from images to video. Readers familiar with any section can skip
it. Section~\ref{sec:delta} is the exception, because it contains the
observation on which the proposed method is built.

\section{Real-time detection and the YOLO family}
\label{sec:yolo}

A single-stage detector predicts object boxes and classes in one pass over
the image, with no separate proposal stage. The YOLO family has three
parts~\citep{wang2024yolov10,tian2025yolov12,yolo26_2026}. A
\emph{backbone} turns the image into feature maps at progressively coarser
resolution. A \emph{neck} mixes information between those resolutions. A
\emph{head} predicts, for each location of each output map, whether an
object is centred there, its box and its class.

The resolution of a feature map is described by its \emph{stride}, the
number of input pixels per feature cell. Following common notation, the map
at stride $2^k$ is called $P_k$. For a $640\times640$ input, $P_3$ has
$80\times80$ cells at stride~8, $P_4$ has $40\times40$ at stride~16 and $P_5$
has $20\times20$ at stride~32. Standard YOLO models predict from $P_3$, $P_4$
and $P_5$. Small-object variants add a $P_2$ head at stride~4, with
$160\times160$ cells~\citep{zhu2021tph,yang2026uavdet}.

The neck follows the feature pyramid network~\citep{lin2017fpn} and its
path-aggregation extension~\citep{liu2018panet}. A top-down path carries
semantic information from coarse maps to fine ones. A bottom-up path then
returns localisation detail to the coarse maps.

Two further design choices matter for this project. The first is
\emph{label assignment}, the rule that decides which predictions are trained
to match which ground-truth objects. Tiny objects cover so few cells that a
poor rule may give them no positive samples at all. YOLO26 introduces a
small-target-aware assignment for this reason~\citep{yolo26_2026}. The second
is \emph{non-maximum suppression} (NMS), which removes duplicate boxes after
prediction. NMS costs time that papers often leave out of latency
figures~\citep{robinson2025rfdetr}. YOLOv10 and YOLO26 offer heads trained
with one-to-one matching that need no NMS~\citep{wang2024yolov10,yolo26_2026}.
Figure~\ref{fig:yolo} summarises the layout and the numbers that recur in
later chapters.

\section{What makes an object small}
\label{sec:small}

Size can be defined in absolute terms, by the number of pixels a box covers,
or relative to the image. Absolute definitions dominate, but the thresholds
differ between benchmarks (Table~\ref{tab:smalldefs}). The COCO thresholds
are the most widely reported. They are too coarse for aerial and long-range
imagery, which is why AI-TOD and SODA subdivide the range below 32 pixels.

Three mechanisms make small objects hard. The first is \emph{sampling}. A
16-pixel object covers half a cell of $P_5$ and four cells of $P_3$. Each
stride-2 downsampling step discards information that later layers cannot
recover. The second is \emph{matching}. Intersection over union (IoU) is
unforgiving at small scales. A box offset by four pixels can drop a 16-pixel
object from IoU 1.0 to below 0.5. The normalised Wasserstein distance was
proposed to soften this~\citep{wang2021nwd}. The third is \emph{context}. A
faint blob may be a pedestrian or noise, and the surrounding scene often
decides which. Global context helps here, and so does temporal context: the
same blob seen moving consistently over several frames. Temporal context is
the lever that Chapter~\ref{ch:method} pulls.

\section{State-space models, from S4 to Mamba}
\label{sec:ssm}

A linear state-space model maps an input signal $x(t)$ to an output $y(t)$
through a hidden state $h(t)\in\R^{N}$:
\begin{equation}
  h'(t) = A\,h(t) + B\,x(t), \qquad y(t) = C\,h(t) + D\,x(t).
  \label{eq:ssm-cont}
\end{equation}
To apply it to a sequence $x_1,\dots,x_L$ sampled with step $\Delta$, the
system is discretised. Under the zero-order hold,
\begin{equation}
  \bar A = \exp(\Delta A), \qquad
  \bar B = (\Delta A)^{-1}\bigl(\exp(\Delta A) - I\bigr)\,\Delta B \;\approx\; \Delta B,
  \label{eq:zoh}
\end{equation}
which gives the recurrence
\begin{equation}
  h_k = \bar A\, h_{k-1} + \bar B\, x_k, \qquad y_k = C\, h_k + D\, x_k .
  \label{eq:ssm-rec}
\end{equation}
When $A$, $B$, $C$ and $\Delta$ do not depend on the input, the output equals
a convolution of the input with the kernel
$\bar K = (C\bar B,\; C\bar A\bar B,\; \dots,\; C\bar A^{L-1}\bar B)$. Structured
state-space models such as S4 exploit this to train in parallel and run
recurrently~\citep{gu2022s4,smith2023s5}.

Mamba makes $B$, $C$ and $\Delta$ functions of the current input,
\begin{equation}
  B_k = W_B\,x_k, \qquad C_k = W_C\,x_k, \qquad
  \Delta_k = \softplus\bigl(w_\Delta^{\top} x_k + b_\Delta\bigr),
  \label{eq:selective}
\end{equation}
so that the model can choose, token by token, what to store and what to
ignore~\citep{gu2023mamba}. The convolutional view is lost. A
hardware-aware parallel scan recovers training speed, and inference
remains a recurrence with a fixed-size state. Cost is linear in sequence
length, against the quadratic cost of self-attention. Mamba-2 later recast
the model as a structured form of linear attention~\citep{dao2024mamba2}. An
independent analysis reached the same view and found that Mamba's forget
gate and block design account for most of its advantage~\citep{han2024mlla}.

\subsection{The discretisation step as a gate}
\label{sec:delta}

In Mamba, $A$ is diagonal with negative real entries. For each channel the
transition $\bar A_k = \exp(\Delta_k A)$ therefore lies between 0 and 1, and
$\Delta_k$ behaves like a gate.
\begin{itemize}
  \item As $\Delta_k \to 0$, $\bar A_k \to I$ and $\bar B_k \to 0$. The state
        is kept unchanged and the input is ignored.
  \item As $\Delta_k \to \infty$, $\bar A_k \to 0$. The past is forgotten and
        the state is rebuilt from the current input.
\end{itemize}
Gu and Dao make the link precise. With a one-dimensional state, $A=-1$ and
$B=1$, the selective recurrence reduces to the gated update
$h_k = (1-g_k)\,h_{k-1} + g_k\,x_k$ with $g_k=\sigma(\text{Linear}(x_k))$
(Theorem~1 of~\citep{gu2023mamba}). Figure~\ref{fig:ssm} shows the recurrence
and the two regimes.

This reading has a second consequence. Equation~\eqref{eq:ssm-cont} is a
continuous-time system, and $\Delta$ is literally a time step. If samples
arrive at irregular intervals, scaling $\Delta$ by the true interval keeps
the discrete model faithful to the same continuous dynamics. Zubi\'c and
colleagues used this property to make event-camera detectors generalise
across sampling frequencies~\citep{zubic2024ssmevent}. The proposed method
uses one control, $\Delta$, for three jobs: weighting sensor reliability,
bridging gaps in a stream and adjusting for frame timing.

\section{State-space models for images}
\label{sec:vssm}

An image is not a sequence, so an SSM must be given an order in which to
read it. The order matters, because a one-dimensional causal scan places
pixels that are adjacent in two dimensions far apart in time. Vision Mamba
(Vim) scans the patch sequence forwards and backwards~\citep{zhu2024vim}.
VMamba's SS2D unrolls the feature map in four directions and merges the
results~\citep{liu2024vmamba}. LocalMamba scans within windows~\citep{huang2024localmamba}.
In 2025 and 2026, Spatial-Mamba~\citep{xiao2025spatialmamba},
DAMamba~\citep{damamba2025}, PRISMamba~\citep{prismamba2026}, VNCT~\citep{vnct2026}
and GraphScan~\citep{graphscan2026} added adaptive and non-directional
alternatives. VNCT goes so far as to argue that directional scanning is
unnecessary. Figure~\ref{fig:scans} shows the main families.

MambaVision is one of the source papers, analysed in
Section~\ref{sec:src-mv}. It takes a hybrid route~\citep{hatamizadeh2025mambavision}.
Its first two stages are convolutional. Its last two alternate a modified
Mamba mixer with self-attention, with attention placed in the later layers.

Two results temper enthusiasm for vision SSMs and must be addressed by any
paper that uses them. MambaOut removed the state-space component from
vision Mamba models and found the result \emph{better} on ImageNet
classification~\citep{yu2025mambaout}. It still trailed VMamba by 1.4 box AP
on COCO detection, so the SSM may earn its place on dense tasks. Second,
Mamba's scan is faster than FlashAttention-2 only beyond about two thousand
tokens~\citep{gu2023mamba,dao2023flash2}. At $640\times640$ input, $P_5$ has
400 tokens and $P_4$ has 1\,600. At those levels, attention or plain
convolution is usually faster in practice, as SF-Mamba reports for short
vision sequences~\citep{sfmamba2026}. Both points shape where \method{}
places its state-space blocks.

\section{Visible--thermal fusion}
\label{sec:fusion}

Visible (RGB) cameras record reflected light, and thermal cameras record
emitted long-wave infrared. Their failure modes are nearly complementary.
RGB is rich in texture and colour but fails in darkness, glare, smoke and
fog. Thermal is indifferent to lighting but flat in texture, and it can
confuse objects that share a temperature with their background. Fusion
methods are usually grouped by where the two streams meet
(Figure~\ref{fig:fusionlevels}):
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{early} or pixel-level fusion stacks the images and feeds one
        network;
  \item \emph{middle} or feature-level fusion runs two backbones and
        exchanges information between their feature maps;
  \item \emph{late} or decision-level fusion runs two detectors and merges
        their boxes~\citep{chen2022proben}.
\end{itemize}
Feature-level fusion dominates current work. It began with CNN and
attention blocks such as CFT~\citep{qingyun2021cft} and
ICAFusion~\citep{shen2024icafusion}. State-space blocks followed: CFMW~\citep{li2025cfmw},
Fusion-Mamba~\citep{dong2025fusionmamba}, COMO~\citep{como2024},
WaveMamba~\citep{wavemamba2025} and MS2Fusion~\citep{ms2fusion2025}.

Three practical problems recur.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Alignment.} The two sensors rarely share an optical axis, so
        their images are offset, and the offset varies with depth. KAIST is
        known for weak alignment~\citep{hwang2015kaist,li2018kaistsanitized}.
        DroneVehicle is only roughly registered~\citep{sun2022dronevehicle}.
        FLIR ADAS is not registered at all, which is why most papers use an
        aligned subset~\citep{zhang2020cfr}.
  \item \emph{Label quality.} Labels drawn on one modality do not always fit
        the other, and some benchmarks have been relabelled for this reason.
  \item \emph{Modality imbalance.} On LLVIP, a thermal-only YOLO comes within
        about one to two points of most fusion methods (Section~\ref{sec:src-cfmw}).
        Fusion results on that dataset therefore say little.
\end{itemize}

\section{From images to video}
\label{sec:video}

Video adds a time axis and three research traditions.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Video object detection} improves per-frame detection by
        aggregating features from neighbouring frames. ImageNet VID is its
        standard benchmark~\citep{russakovsky2015imagenet}. Most methods use a
        window of past and future frames, which is acceptable offline but
        not in a live stream. TMambaDet (CVPR 2026) is a recent example,
        using a bidirectional temporal Mamba~\citep{tmambadet2026}.
  \item \emph{Streaming perception} recognises that the world moves while
        the detector computes. It scores predictions against the state of
        the world at the moment they are delivered~\citep{li2020streaming}.
        StreamYOLO, LongShortNet and DAMO-StreamNet forecast object motion to
        compensate~\citep{yang2022streamyolo,li2023longshortnet,he2023damostreamnet}.
  \item \emph{Multi-object tracking} links detections into identities over
        time. Tracking-by-detection methods associate boxes with motion and
        appearance cues: SORT-family trackers, ByteTrack, BoT-SORT,
        OC-SORT and TrackTrack~\citep{zhang2022bytetrack,aharon2022botsort,cao2023ocsort,shim2025tracktrack}.
        End-to-end trackers carry learned queries from frame to frame:
        MOTR, MOTIP and the state-space SambaMOTR~\citep{zhang2023motrv2,gao2025motip,segu2025sambamotr}.
        Accuracy is measured with HOTA~\citep{luiten2021hota} and
        IDF1~\citep{ristani2016idf1}.
\end{itemize}
A causal detector, one that uses only the past, is the natural fit for a
live camera. A recurrent state is the most economical way to give it
memory. Recurrent detectors exist for event cameras, using recurrent vision
transformers~\citep{gehrig2023rvt} and state-space
models~\citep{zubic2024ssmevent}. Chapter~\ref{ch:novelty} shows that the
equivalent for visible--thermal video of small objects has not been
published.
