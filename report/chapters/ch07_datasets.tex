\chapter{Datasets}
\label{ch:data}

The datasets decide what the paper can claim. This chapter sets out the
criteria for choosing them and the recommended core stack. It describes the
main benchmark in detail and lists the supporting datasets by role. It then
covers licences, datasets that must not be used, and the data engineering
that turns downloads into reproducible inputs.

\section{Selection criteria}

A dataset enters the plan only if it meets five criteria.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Standing.} It is published at a recognised venue and used as a
        benchmark by the work the paper will be compared against.
  \item \emph{Relevance.} It contributes small objects, paired visible and
        thermal imagery, or temporal sequences. The best datasets contribute
        all three.
  \item \emph{Label quality.} Known label problems have published fixes,
        such as the corrected LLVIP labels and the sanitised KAIST labels.
  \item \emph{Licence.} It permits research use. All datasets used here
        permit it, and most forbid commercial use.
  \item \emph{Feasibility.} It can be stored, converted and trained on within
        the Kaggle and local budget of Section~\ref{sec:compute}.
\end{enumerate}

\section{The main benchmark: RGBT-Tiny}
\label{sec:rgbttiny}

RGBT-Tiny, published in IEEE TPAMI in 2025, is the single best match for
this project~\citep{ying2025rgbttiny}.
\begin{itemize}
  \item It contains 115 paired visible--thermal sequences: 93\,000 frames
        and 1.2 million boxes in seven classes. The classes are ship, car,
        cyclist, pedestrian, bus, drone and plane.
  \item More than 81\% of its objects are smaller than $16\times16$ pixels.
  \item The frames are $640\times512$, aligned by homography, at 15 frames per
        second, across eight scene types.
  \item The official split is 85 training sequences (79\,050 frames) and 30
        test sequences (13\,950 frames).
  \item Boxes carry track identities, and the companion VT-Tiny-MOT
        benchmark provides tracking labels on the same
        sequences~\citep{hgttrack2024}. Phases~1 to~3 can therefore all be
        evaluated on one data source.
  \item It proposes SAFit, a matching measure intended to be fairer than IoU
        for tiny boxes. SAFit is reported alongside standard AP.
\end{itemize}

Two cautions apply. Access is granted through a request form, so the
request must be made at the start of Phase~0. Its alignment is by
homography, so residual parallax remains at short range. That is a feature
for testing the offset-guided alignment of the CMFM block. It is also a
reason to audit the alignment before training (Section~\ref{sec:audit}).

\section{Licences}
\label{sec:licences}

Almost every dataset in the plan is licensed for non-commercial research
only. That is sufficient for a thesis and a journal article. Four further
rules follow from the terms.
\begin{itemize}
  \item The data must not be redistributed. Kaggle datasets created from it
        must be private.
  \item The images of MSMT17 must not be shown in publications, at the
        authors' request.
  \item Any commercial follow-up would need datasets with permissive terms,
        such as SeaDronesSee (CC0) and MEVA (CC BY 4.0), or newly collected
        data.
  \item The Ultralytics library is licensed under AGPL-3.0. A public release
        of code built on it must use the same licence~\citep{yolo26_2026}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Datasets that must not be used}
\label{sec:withdrawn}

Several surveillance datasets were withdrawn after it emerged that people
had been recorded without adequate consent (Table~\ref{tab:withdrawn}).
Withdrawal did not stop their use. Peng, Mathur and Narayanan documented
continued use of DukeMTMC and its derivatives in hundreds of papers after
retraction~\citep{peng2021stewardship}. A project inspired by a surveillance
fiction must be scrupulous here.

\section{Data engineering}
\label{sec:dataeng}

Figure~\ref{fig:datapipe} shows the path from downloads to training inputs.
Five practices make the results reproducible.

\paragraph{Immutable raw data}
Archives are stored as downloaded, with checksums recorded in a dataset
card. Every later step is a script that reads raw data and writes derived
data. Derived data can always be regenerated.

\paragraph{One paired format}
Visible and thermal frames are stored in two folders with identical file
names. A single label file per frame is expressed in the visible frame's
coordinates. Ultralytics' multi-channel input stacks the channels into one
TIFF and is convenient for early fusion~\citep{yolo26_2026}. The two-folder
layout is used for middle fusion because the backbones are separate.

\paragraph{Alignment audit}
\label{sec:audit}
For each visible--thermal dataset, the residual offset between modalities is
measured on a sample of frames. The measurement uses phase correlation of
edge maps, and the distribution is reported. The audit tells the paper how
aligned each benchmark really is. That calibrates the synthetic misalignment
used in training and evaluation.

\paragraph{Leakage-free splits}
Frames from one video are strongly correlated. Splits are therefore made at
the sequence level, and official splits are used wherever they exist. No
sequence may contribute frames to both training and test sets.

\paragraph{Packaging for Kaggle}
Converted datasets are uploaded as private Kaggle datasets, versioned and
shared across notebooks, so that each training session starts without
re-downloading. Ignore regions, such as VisDrone's, are carried into
evaluation and not silently dropped.
