\chapter{Stage four: seeing in two spectra}

\section{Why it matters}

A visible camera sees colour and texture but goes blind at night, in glare and in smoke; a thermal camera sees
heat in any light but loses texture and colour. The question the project asks is how to combine them so the
result is better than either alone, and stays good when one of them fails. This stage covers the data, the
fusion designs that came before \method{}, and how they are evaluated.

\section{What to learn}

\begin{itemize}
  \item What a long-wave infrared image measures, and why people and engines stand out in it.
  \item Registration: why the two cameras must be aligned, and what a few pixels of misalignment does to a detector.
  \item Early, middle and late fusion; where \method{} fuses and why.
  \item Attention-based fusion~\cite{qingyun2021cft,shen2024icafusion} and Mamba-based fusion~\cite{dong2025fusionmamba,li2025cfmw,como2024}.
  \item The datasets: KAIST~\cite{hwang2015kaist}, LLVIP~\cite{jia2021llvip}, M3FD~\cite{liu2022tardal} and RGBT-Tiny~\cite{ying2025rgbttiny};
        what each is good for and what each hides.
  \item Robustness testing: dropping, darkening or shifting one camera at test time, and modality dropout at training time.
\end{itemize}

\section{Exercises}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Load ten LLVIP pairs, overlay visible and thermal at 50 per cent, and judge the registration by eye.
  \item Shift the thermal image by 8 pixels and measure how much the concat baseline's detections move.
  \item From notebook 09, explain why concat collapses when told that thermal is gone while \method{} does not.
\end{enumerate}
