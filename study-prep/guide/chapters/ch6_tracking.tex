\chapter{Stage five: tracking and video}

\section{Why it matters}

A detector answers \emph{what is here}; a tracker answers \emph{who is this, and where are they going}. The
showcase already tracks crowds, and the project's next phase moves from still images to video, where \method{}'s
temporal state memory carries information from frame to frame. This stage covers the tracking-by-detection
pipeline and how it is measured.

\section{What to learn}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Tracking by detection: detect in every frame, then associate detections into identities.
  \item The Kalman filter as a constant-velocity motion model, and the Hungarian algorithm for matching~\cite{bewley2016sort}.
  \item Using low-confidence detections to keep tracks through occlusion~\cite{zhang2022bytetrack}.
  \item Fixing drift and camera motion~\cite{cao2023ocsort,aharon2022botsort}.
  \item Metrics: MOTA, IDF1 and HOTA, and the MOT benchmarks~\cite{milan2016mot16}.
  \item Visible--thermal tracking data~\cite{vtmot2024} and spatio-temporal fusion~\cite{mambast2024}.
  \item Why sampled frames cannot be tracked: the LLVIP test frames are seconds apart, which is why the showcase uses MOT17 video.
\end{itemize}

\section{Exercises}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Implement a Kalman filter for one moving box and plot predicted against observed positions.
  \item Track MOT17-04 with and without low-confidence association and count identity switches.
  \item Explain why 72 identities over 150 frames of MOT17-04 is plausible and 115 is not.
\end{enumerate}
