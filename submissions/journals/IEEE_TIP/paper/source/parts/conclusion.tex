\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
CFFM-Net places the decision about camera trust where a state-space model writes its state: a per-pixel reliability for each camera scales the step of a four-direction scan over interleaved visible and thermal tokens, on top of a stock YOLO26 detector. At nano scale and over three seeds, fusing the two cameras beats the best single camera on M3FD by \gainMfdCffmVtwoOverBestMeanMap{}\,mAP, but most of that is available to a plain concatenation baseline, which gains \gainMfdConcatMdOverBestMeanMap{}\,mAP. The gated scan adds \marMfdVtwoVsConcatMdMeanMap{}\,$\pm$\,\marMfdVtwoVsConcatMdStdMap{}\,mAP on M3FD, not significant at $n=3$ (paired $p=\sigMfdP{}$), and nothing measurable on LLVIP or on KAIST stills. Its advantage under thermal-side faults on M3FD is consistent in direction, but a baseline trained with fault augmentation is more robust than the model, and the reliability loss of v3 lowers accuracy. The Temporal State Memory cuts the KAIST miss rate under thermal noise from \memThermalNoiseImageAllMr{}\,\% to \memThermalNoiseMemoryAllMr{}\,\%, with a confound we could not remove, helps little when the thermal camera is lost, and does not improve tracking. The live runtime holds delay and GPU memory constant over ten minutes at \livSoakThirtyFps{} frames per second on a nominal 30. The multi-camera layer, demonstrated with an RGB-only detector on WILDTRACK, reaches \camDetFineTunedModa{}\,\% MODA, \camTrackMota{}\,\% MOTA and a count error of \camCountRelErr{}\,\%.

\section*{Declaration}
No hyperparameter was tuned on a test split. The one design decision that used evaluation results was made on LLVIP at seed zero: the P2 ablations of round one led us to drop the stride-4 level in v2 (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}); these ablations were not run on M3FD, and the decision was applied unchanged to M3FD and to seeds one and two. The M3FD hold-out split is fixed and shared by every run, and the fault benchmark scores checkpoints without being used to select a model.
