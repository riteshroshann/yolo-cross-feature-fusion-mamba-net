\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
\subsection{Real-time detectors and small objects}
Real-time detection rests on feature pyramids~\cite{lin2017fpn} and path aggregation~\cite{liu2018panet}, and the YOLO family, most recently YOLO26~\cite{yolo26_2026} and its predecessor YOLO11~\cite{yolo11_2024}, packages both with a one-stage head. Small objects remain the acknowledged failure case. HPS-DETR~\cite{wang2025hpsdetr} and CFMW~\cite{li2025cfmw} both report that small objects are still missed.

\subsection{State-space models in vision}
Mamba makes the state-space step input dependent~\cite{gu2023mamba}. Vision variants differ mainly in how they order two-dimensional tokens: bidirectional scans~\cite{zhu2024vim}, cross scans over four directions~\cite{liu2024vmamba}. Mamba YOLO brings the idea into a YOLO detector~\cite{wang2025mambayolo}, and UAVDet pairs convolution with Mamba for small objects in aerial imagery~\cite{yang2026uavdet}. Sequential state updates use GPU parallelism poorly, and CFMW reports a large rise in computation from its scan; our cost results in Section~\ref{sec:cost} quantify the price for a four-direction scan with a small state (size \cfgDState{}).

\subsection{Visible-thermal object detection}
Multispectral pedestrian detection grew up on KAIST~\cite{hwang2015kaist}, where early work compared where the two streams should meet~\cite{liu2016halfway}, added a segmentation branch~\cite{li2018msdsrcnn}, addressed misalignment~\cite{zhang2019arcnn} and built interaction and guidance modules on two-stream convolutional detectors~\cite{zhang2019cian,zhang2021gaff,cao2023csaa}. More recent detectors on KAIST, LLVIP and M3FD fall into three groups. Transformer fusers attend across the two modalities inside a YOLO-style or two-stage detector: CFT~\cite{fang2021cft} and ICAFusion~\cite{shen2024icafusion}, and IRDFusion with iterative relation-map differences~\cite{shen2025irdfusion}. Detection transformers adapt the DETR family to two inputs, as in MS-DETR~\cite{xing2024msdetr}, DAMSDet~\cite{guo2024damsdet} and GM-DETR~\cite{xiao2024gmdetr}. Other designs change the pipeline around the detector: a single-stage multispectral framework built on YOLOv11~\cite{wan2025yolov11rgbt}, a target-aware fusion for pedestrians~\cite{zhang2024tfdet}, an end-to-end design that trains image fusion and detection together~\cite{zhang2024e2emfd}, and a coarse-to-fine removal and selection scheme~\cite{zhao2026rsdet}. We compare with the published numbers of several of these in Section~\ref{sec:sota} and do not re-run them.

State-space fusion has appeared in this setting recently. Fusion-Mamba builds cross-modality fusion modules from state-space blocks~\cite{dong2025fusionmamba}, CFMW targets adverse weather~\cite{li2025cfmw}, WaveMamba applies a wavelet-driven Mamba fusion~\cite{zhu2025wavemamba}, and DMM uses disparity guidance for oriented objects in remote sensing~\cite{zhou2024dmm}. As far as their titles and abstracts show, these designs use the scan to mix the two modalities; we did not find one that gates the step of the scan by a per-camera reliability. MambaST fuses a few consecutive frames of a visible-thermal pair for pedestrian detection~\cite{mambast2024}; our Temporal State Memory differs in carrying a recurrent state indefinitely and in tying its decay to sensor reliability and real elapsed time.

\subsection{Reliability, illumination and modality imbalance}
The idea that one camera can be less trustworthy than the other is not new. MBNet argues that multispectral detectors suffer from modality imbalance and uses illumination information to align and weight the two feature streams~\cite{zhou2020mbnet}, and MS-DETR adds a modality-balanced optimisation to its loosely coupled fusion~\cite{xing2024msdetr}. In the works we surveyed, reliability or illumination weights the modalities on the features or on the output of the fusion. We found no method that controls the discretisation step of the scan itself per location and per modality, which is the design choice we test. In the works we surveyed we also found no benchmark that corrupts one camera of a pair with a range of physical faults, which is why Section~\ref{sec:experiments} builds one.

\subsection{Datasets and tracking}
KAIST is the classic multispectral pedestrian benchmark, with sanitised annotations available~\cite{li2018kaistsanitized}, and its numbers depend strongly on whether training and test annotations are original or sanitised~\cite{zhang2021gaff}. WILDTRACK supplies calibrated, synchronised multi-camera video of pedestrians~\cite{chavdarova2018wildtrack}. For tracking by detection we use ByteTrack, which associates low-confidence boxes as well as confident ones~\cite{zhang2022bytetrack}.
